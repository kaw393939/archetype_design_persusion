# Punk
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
British punk graphics grew with the music scene between about 1976 and 1979. The style is closely tied to the Sex Pistols, their manager Malcolm McLaren, and designer Jamie Reid. It rejected the neat order and clarity valued by modernist design. Instead, punk designers borrowed familiar public images, cut them apart, and returned them in mocking or confrontational forms.

Reid's approach grew from radical politics and the Situationist International. He developed it while studying at art college in Croydon and through *Suburban Press*, a magazine he published there from 1970 to 1975. Cheap reproduction methods suited the movement. Photocopies, rough collage, and found newspaper type made graphics feel immediate and possible to produce outside a professional studio.

## How do you recognize or use it?
Punk graphics often use cut-and-paste construction, ransom-note lettering made from newspaper type, torn edges, and visible photocopy grain. Official symbols such as the Queen's portrait or the Union flag may be covered, cropped, or defaced. Fluorescent colors clash on purpose. Alignment can feel improvised, yet the main message is usually forceful.

This style works when a project needs an anti-establishment voice or a handmade sense of protest. Use its roughness selectively. If every line uses mixed lettering, readers may struggle to find the offer or action.

### How to use it in a hero
Arrange torn-paper or collage panels around the main image. Use mixed cut-out type for the headline only. Keep body copy and the CTA readable on a plain block, with enough contrast and spacing to make the action clear.

## Examples
### Jamie Reid, *God Save the Queen*
This 1977 poster is an offset lithographic print on paper measuring 60 by 38 cm. It places a black and white photograph of Queen Elizabeth II over a Union flag. Strips carrying the song title and band name cover her eyes and mouth in ransom-note lettering. Released during the Queen's Silver Jubilee in June 1977, the poster turns official national imagery into a provocative music advertisement. Institution: Victoria and Albert Museum, London, Theatre and Performance Collection, museum number S.1286-1982. [V&A record](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1159869/god-save-the-queen-poster-reid-jamie).

### Jamie Reid, *Never Mind the Bollocks Here's The Sex Pistols*
Reid designed this 1977 record sleeve, issued by Virgin Records Ltd as an offset color lithograph. Fluorescent yellow and pink create an abrasive field for mismatched ransom-note type. There is no photograph to soften the statement, so color and lettering carry the identity. Institution: Victoria and Albert Museum, museum number E.1829-1992. [V&A record](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O148280). A related object is the [V&A badge S.938-1990](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1152783).
