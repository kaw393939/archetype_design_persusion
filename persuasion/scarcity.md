# Scarcity
[Back to this section](README.md) · [Home](../README.md)

## What is it?
Scarcity is one of Robert Cialdini's principles of persuasion. People tend to value an option more when it is rare, running out, or available for a limited time. A restriction can make the option feel more desirable because losing access often feels more painful than gaining the same opportunity feels rewarding.

Scarcity can concern quantity, time, access, or production. A product may come from a genuinely limited batch, an event may have a real registration deadline, or a service may accept only as much work as its staff can complete. The limit needs to be specific enough for a reader to understand and truthful enough for the seller to defend.

Research by Stephen Worchel, Jerry Lee, and Akanbi Adewole examined how supply affects perceived value. In their 1975 cookie-jar study, cookies presented in a jar containing two were rated as more desirable than the same cookies presented in a jar containing ten. The difference came from apparent availability rather than a change in the cookies themselves.

## How do you recognize or use it?
Scarcity messages often mention a deadline, a remaining supply, a limited edition, or access restricted by a real condition. Effective use answers two questions: what is limited, and why is it limited? Clear reasons, such as a seasonal production schedule or a venue's capacity, help the message feel credible.

Place the limit near the choice it affects. A supporting line can explain the production condition, while the CTA states the immediate action. Avoid adding pressure to every element on the page. The product still needs a clear description so a customer can decide whether it fits.

Ethics: only claim a limit that is true; fake countdowns and false "only 2 left" messages damage trust. A limit should reflect actual inventory, timing, or capacity and should disappear when it no longer applies.

## Examples
### Hypothetical example for our team's practice brand

Headline: One batch of the white tee, made for this season.

Supporting line: We produce one seasonal batch and close orders when that batch is allocated.

CTA: "Check batch availability" goes to the product page, where current availability and the production policy are explained.

The seasonal batch is the scarce condition, and the shop would have to follow that production rule in practice. The wording gives a reason for the limit without claiming buyer numbers or pretending that stock is lower than it is. The CTA lets the customer verify the current state before making a purchase.
